It’s easy for a small project to become a long list of possibilities. Each new idea sounds useful on its own. Together, they can make it harder to see what the project is really for.

## Ask one question at a time

Before adding something new, we try to ask who it helps and what it makes easier. A clear answer is usually more useful than a long feature list.

1. Describe the problem in a sentence.
2. Make the smallest useful improvement.
3. Give it time before deciding what comes next.

This is a way to keep our attention on the work in front of us.

## Make the everyday things easy

Good work often lives in ordinary details: a heading that makes sense, a link that takes you where you expect, or a paragraph that answers your question.

**Simple can still feel complete.** It can mean that someone has taken the time to decide what belongs.

We’re learning to leave a little more space, and to use it well.
